Transistor Level-Shifting Circuit

Step-by-step wiring guide — each step has its own diagram + real reference photos

0 Identify Your Parts

Grab these from the Smraza kit:

  • 2× 2N2222 transistors — small black plastic with 3 metal legs and flat face with text
  • 2× 1K resistors — tiny cylinder with color bands: Brown-Black-Red-Gold
  • 5× Female-to-male DuPont wires — one end has a socket (goes on Pi pin), other is a pin (goes in breadboard)
  • 4× Male-to-male jumper wires — pins on both ends (breadboard to breadboard or breadboard to driver)
  • 1× Breadboard
1 Identify the Transistor Pins (CRITICAL)
⚠ FIRST: Read the text printed on your transistor! Different variants have different pinouts:
  • If it says "2N2222A" or "PN2222A": Pin order is E-B-C (Emitter left, Collector right)
  • If it says "P2N2222A": Pin order is C-B-E (Collector left, Emitter right) — REVERSED!

The Smraza kit most likely has 2N2222A (E-B-C). Check the text on yours to be sure.

Real Reference Photo (2N2222A)

This is what the actual component looks like. Hold it with flat side facing you, legs pointing down:

Source: components101.com

For the 2N2222A (most common), the three pins from left to right are:

  • Pin 1 (LEFT) = Emitter (E) — connects to ground
  • Pin 2 (MIDDLE) = Base (B) — connects to resistor
  • Pin 3 (RIGHT) = Collector (C) — connects to driver
P2N2222A variant (reversed pinout): If your transistor says P2N2222A, just swap the Emitter and Collector positions — put Collector in Col A (left) and Emitter in Col C (right). Base stays in the middle. The breadboard row connections to GND and driver stay the same positions (Col A → GND, Col C → driver).
2 Locate the 5 Pi Pins You Need

Look at the Pi with USB ports facing down/toward you. The GPIO header is the 2×20 row of pins at the top edge.

You need exactly 5 pins:

  • Pin 2 (5V) — top row, 1st from left
  • Pin 4 (5V) — top row, 2nd from left
  • Pin 6 (GND) — top row, 3rd from left
  • Pin 11 (GPIO17) — bottom row, 6th from left
  • Pin 13 (GPIO27) — bottom row, 7th from left

Pins zigzag: top row = even (2,4,6,8...), bottom row = odd (1,3,5,7...)

! Complete Breadboard Map — Where Everything Goes

This shows your breadboard in the same horizontal orientation as your real board. Orient your board to match this exactly. Steps A-L below walk you through it one piece at a time.

How to orient your breadboard: Hold it horizontally (long side left-right). Row numbers (1, 2, 3...) go left to right along the center channel. Column letters are on the left side — upper half: j,i,h,g,f (we don't use these), lower half: e,d,c,b,a (our components go here). Blue (-) lines are on the outer edges, Red (+) lines on the inner edges. We only use the bottom blue (-) rail for ground.
A Connect Pi Ground to Breadboard

Take a female-to-male DuPont wire.

  • Plug the female (socket) end onto Pi Pin 6 (GND)
  • Plug the male (pin) end into the blue GND rail (−) on the breadboard — any hole in the bottom rail

This is the shared ground for both transistors.

B Place Transistor 1 (PUL) in Row 5

Take the first 2N2222 transistor.

Hold it with the flat side facing you. Push the 3 legs into Row 5:

  • Col A (left hole) = Emitter
  • Col B (next hole) = Base
  • Col C (next hole) = Collector

Leave Col D and E empty for now.

C Wire T1 Emitter to Ground Rail

Take a short male-to-male jumper wire.

  • One end in any empty hole on Row 5 (like Col E — it's connected to the Emitter internally)
  • Other end in the GND rail (−) directly below

This grounds the Emitter of Transistor 1.

D Place Resistor 1 (Row 3 ↔ Row 5)

Take a 1K resistor (Brown-Black-Red-Gold).

Bend the legs and insert:

  • One leg into Row 3, Col D
  • Other leg into Row 5, Col D

The resistor bridges Row 3 to Row 5. Since all holes in Row 5 (A–E) connect internally, this connects through to the Base (Col B). The resistor limits current to protect the transistor.

E Connect Pi GPIO 17 (Pin 11) → Row 3, Col E

Take a female-to-male DuPont wire.

  • Female end onto Pi Pin 11 (GPIO 17)
  • Male end into Row 3, Col E on the breadboard

Signal path: Pi GPIO17 → Row 3 E → (through Row 3 to D) → through R1 → Row 5 → transistor Base.

F Wire T1 Collector → Driver PUL−

Take a male-to-male jumper wire (or longer wire if needed).

  • One end in any empty hole on Row 5 (like Col E) — it's connected to Collector at Col C
  • Other end into the PUL− screw terminal on the 2HSS86 driver

Tighten the screw terminal firmly.

G Place Transistor 2 (DIR) in Row 10

Take the second 2N2222 transistor. Same orientation — flat side facing you.

  • Row 10, Col A = Emitter
  • Row 10, Col B = Base
  • Row 10, Col C = Collector
H Wire T2 Emitter to Ground Rail

Short male-to-male jumper:

  • One end in Row 10 (any open hole) → GND rail (−)

Same as Step C, but for Transistor 2.

I Place Resistor 2 (Row 8 ↔ Row 10)

Second 1K resistor:

  • One leg → Row 8, Col D
  • Other leg → Row 10, Col D

Same as Step D but for the direction circuit. Bridges the signal from Row 8 down to the transistor base in Row 10.

J Connect Pi GPIO 27 (Pin 13) → Row 8, Col E

Female-to-male DuPont wire:

  • Female end onto Pi Pin 13 (GPIO 27)
  • Male end into Row 8, Col E

Direction signal: Pi → Row 8 E → (through Row 8 to D) → R2 → Row 10 → transistor 2 Base.

K Wire T2 Collector → Driver DIR−

Male-to-male jumper wire:

  • One end in Row 10 (connected to Collector at Col C)
  • Other end → DIR− screw terminal on driver
L Connect 5V Power: Pi → Driver PUL+ and DIR+

Two female-to-male DuPont wires go directly from Pi to driver (no breadboard needed):

  • Pi Pin 2 (5V) → PUL+ terminal on driver
  • Pi Pin 4 (5V) → DIR+ terminal on driver

These provide the 5V that powers the optocouplers inside the driver. The transistors on the breadboard switch the ground side (PUL−/DIR−).

You're Done Wiring!

✓ Final check — count all 13 connections:
  • Pi Pin 6 (GND) → Breadboard GND rail
  • Transistor 1 in Row 5 (flat side toward you)
  • Row 5 Col A (T1 emitter) → GND rail
  • 1K Resistor 1: Row 3 Col D ↔ Row 5 Col D
  • Pi Pin 11 (GPIO 17) → Row 3 Col E
  • Row 5 Col C (T1 collector) → Driver PUL−
  • Transistor 2 in Row 10 (flat side toward you)
  • Row 10 Col A (T2 emitter) → GND rail
  • 1K Resistor 2: Row 8 Col D ↔ Row 10 Col D
  • Pi Pin 13 (GPIO 27) → Row 8 Col E
  • Row 10 Col C (T2 collector) → Driver DIR−
  • Pi Pin 2 (5V) → Driver PUL+
  • Pi Pin 4 (5V) → Driver DIR+
