Before You Start — Two Things to Know
Transistor pinout + how the breadboard works

1. Transistor Pins

Hold it with the flat side facing you, legs pointing down:

         FLAT SIDE (you can read the text)
         ┌───────────────────┐
         │      2N2222       │
         └───────────────────┘
           │       │       │
           E       B       C
        LEFT    MIDDLE    RIGHT
       EMITTER   BASE   COLLECTOR

2. How YOUR Breadboard is Laid Out

Hold the board with the letters a-e on the bottom half and f-j on the top half. Row numbers go left (1) to right (30). The blue ground rails are on the outer edges (very top and very bottom of the board).

  ━ BLUE (–) ground rail ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━   ← very top edge
  ━ RED (+) power rail ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

     f  g  h  i  j     ← top half (rows go 1→30 left to right)
     ·  ·  ·  ·  ·
     ─────────────      ← CENTER GAP
     a  b  c  d  e     ← bottom half (WE USE THIS HALF)
     ·  ·  ·  ·  ·

  ━ RED (+) power rail ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  ━ BLUE (–) ground rail ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━   ← very bottom edge

3. Key Rule

Each transistor leg goes in a different row. We spread the legs apart so each leg gets its own row. Because all 5 holes in a row (a through e) are connected underneath, putting a wire in any col of a row connects to everything else in that row. This gives us room for all our wires.

1
Place Transistor 1 (PULSE)
Spread legs into rows 4, 5, 6 — column a

What to do

Take one 2N2222 transistor. Hold it with flat side facing you. Gently spread the legs apart so they're about 2.5mm (one row) apart. Then push each leg into column a:


Emitter (left leg) → Row 4, col a

Base (middle leg) → Row 5, col a

Collector (right leg) → Row 6, col a 

     a     b     c     d     e
  3  ·     ·     ·     ·     ·
  4  [E]    ·     ·     ·     ·     ← Emitter (left leg)
  5  [B]    ·     ·     ·     ·     ← Base (middle leg)
  6  [C]    ·     ·     ·     ·     ← Collector (right leg)
  7  ·     ·     ·     ·     ·
The transistor body will hover above the board between rows 4-6. The legs go straight down into column a. This is normal.

3
Connect Emitter 1 to Ground
Red short wire from Row 4 col e → blue minus rail above

Find the ground rail

Look at the very bottom edge of your breadboard — below the a-e rows. You'll see a strip of holes along a blue line with a minus sign (–). That's the ground rail. Every hole along that blue line is connected to every other hole on it.

What to do

Take a short RED male-to-male wire.


One end → Row 4, col e (this is the emitter's row — col e connects to the emitter in col a)

Other end → Any hole on the BOTTOM blue – rail (the one closest to the a-e rows, pick the hole nearest to row 4) 

     a     b     c     d     e
  3  ·     ·     ·     ·     ·
  4  [E]    ·     ·     ·    [●]   ← red wire goes here (row 4 col e)
  5  [B]    ·     ·     ·     ·            │
  6  [C]    ·     ·     ·     ·            │  red wire
                                          │  (goes DOWN)
  ━ RED (+) power rail ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━  │
  ━ BLUE (–) ━  ·   ·   ·  [●]  ·   ·   ·  ← other end here
  GROUND RAIL (very bottom edge)

Why this works

Row 4 holes a through e are all connected underneath. The emitter leg is in col a. Your wire in col e is electrically connected to the emitter through the row. The other end goes down to the ground rail at the bottom. Result: emitter is grounded.

Check yourself

You should now have 2 red wires making short bridges from the main grid up to the blue ground rail. One near row 4, one near row 14.

5
Place Resistor 1
Blue resistor bridges Row 2 col c to Row 5 col c

What to do

Take one blue 1K resistor. Bend the legs so they're about 3 rows apart. Insert:


One leg → Row 2, col c (this row will receive the Pi signal)

Other leg → Row 5, col c (this is the Base row — connects to the base in col a) 

     a     b     c     d     e
  2  ·     ·    [R1]    ·     ·     ← resistor top (Pi signal enters here)
  3  ·     ·     │     ·     ·
  4  [E]    ·     │     ·    [gnd]
  5  [B]    ·    [R1]    ·     ·     ← resistor bottom (connects to base)
  6  [C]    ·     ·     ·     ·

Why this works

The resistor bridges from Row 2 to Row 5. The base leg is in Row 5 col a. Since all of Row 5 (a-e) is connected, the resistor in col c connects to the base in col a. The Pi signal will enter Row 2, go through the resistor, and reach the base.

7
Pi Ground → Breadboard Ground Rail
Blue short wire: Pi Pin 6 → blue minus rail

What to do

Take a male-to-female jumper wire from your rainbow kit (any color — suggest black or green).


Female end → Pi Physical Pin 6 (GND)

Male end → Bottom blue minus (–) rail on breadboard (any hole on it)

Finding Pin 6

Orient the Pi with the USB ports facing away from you. The GPIO header is the double row of 40 pins. Pin 6 is the 3rd pin from the top on the RIGHT column:

  GPIO HEADER (USB ports away from you)

  Left col             Right col
  ────────             ─────────
  (1)  3.3V    ●    ●    (2)  5V
  (3)  GPIO2   ●    ●    (4)  5V
  (5)  GPIO3   ●    ●    (6)  GND  ◄── THIS PIN
  (7)  GPIO4   ●    ●    (8)  TX
  (9)  GND     ●    ●    (10) RX
  (11) GPIO17  ●    ●    (12) GPIO18
  (13) GPIO27  ●    ●    (14) GND

  Count from top-right: (2)... (4)... (6) = third pin down
8
Pi Pin 11 (PULSE signal) → Breadboard
Male-to-female wire → Row 2, col e

What to do

Take a male-to-female jumper wire from your rainbow kit (suggest YELLOW so you can identify the pulse signal).


Female end → Pi Physical Pin 11 (GPIO 17)

Male end → Row 2, col e on breadboard

Finding Pin 11

6th pin from the top on the LEFT column. Count: (1), (3), (5), (7), (9), (11).

  (9)  GND     ●    ●    (10) RX
  (11) GPIO17  ◄── THIS PIN    ●    (12) GPIO18
  (13) GPIO27  ●    ●    (14) GND

Signal path

Pi GPIO 17 → wire → Row 2 col e → (Row 2 is all connected) → Resistor top in Row 2 col c → through resistor → Row 5 col c → (Row 5 is all connected) → Base in Row 5 col a → transistor receives the pulse signal!

  (11) GPIO17  ●    ●    (12) GPIO18

10
Driver PUL- (green wire) → Breadboard
Green wire from driver → Row 6, col e

What to do

The GREEN wire that's already plugged into PUL- on the 2HSS86 driver — take its other end and plug it into:


Row 6, col e

Why Row 6?

Row 6 is the collector row for Transistor 1. The collector leg is in Row 6 col a. Your wire in col e connects to the collector through the row. When the transistor turns ON, current flows from PUL- through the collector to the emitter (ground), completing the circuit.

11
Driver DIR- (green wire) → Breadboard
Green wire from driver → Row 16, col e

What to do

The other GREEN wire from the driver's DIR- terminal — plug its other end into:


Row 16, col e

Why Row 16?

Row 16 = Transistor 2's collector row. Same logic as step 10.

12
Driver PUL+ (black wire) → Pi 5V
Black wire from driver → Pi Pin 2

What to do

The BLACK wire from the driver's PUL+ terminal — plug its female end onto:


Pi Physical Pin 2 (5V) — 1st pin on the RIGHT column, very top

  (1)  3.3V    ●    ●    (2)  5V  ◄── BLACK wire here
  (3)  GPIO2   ●    ●    (4)  5V
13
Driver DIR+ (blue wire) → Pi 5V
Blue wire from driver → Pi Pin 4

What to do

The BLUE wire from the driver's DIR+ terminal — plug its female end onto:


Pi Physical Pin 4 (5V) — 2nd pin on the RIGHT column

  (1)  3.3V    ●    ●    (2)  5V   ← PUL+ black
  (3)  GPIO2   ●    ●    (4)  5V  ◄── BLUE wire here
